# 90-Day Master Editor Revenue Plan

## Primary Objective

Move Master Editor from approximately **1,000–1,500/month** toward a more consistent **$5,000/month run rate** by monetizing existing YouTube demand and email traffic.

This plan does **not** depend on viral growth. It depends on:

- Converting high-intent YouTube viewers into email subscribers.
- Reactivating the existing email list.
- Selling a practical beginner product.
- Moving qualified beginners into **Audiobook Voice-Over Launchpad**.
- Offering **Premium Consulting** to people who need individualized diagnosis, feedback, or workflow correction.
- Tracking which videos, emails, and offers produce leads and sales.

Revenue is not guaranteed. The objective is to build a repeatable system and use the data to improve it.

# 2. The Coherent Value Ladder

Do not promote unrelated products simultaneously. Every video, email, and workshop should lead people through this sequence:

## Step 1: Free Lead Magnet

### Recommended lead magnet

**Professional Home Recording and ACX Audio Checklist**

Include:

- Microphone and room setup.
- Recording level checklist.
- 10-second test-recording procedure.
- Noise and room-tone check.
- Silence-gap editing checklist.
- Basic Audacity processing order.
- Loudness and peak checks.
- ACX-related quality checks.
- Final export checklist.
- Common mistakes that make narration sound amateur.

The checklist should help people make a quick improvement, but not replace the need for instruction.

### CTA

> Download the free Professional Home Recording and ACX Audio Checklist. It will help you check your recording setup, levels, noise, silence gaps, and final export before you lose hours editing the wrong way.

## Step 2: Low-Ticket Starter Product or Workshop

This should solve one immediate problem and produce a visible result.

Possible formats:

- **Clean Voice Starter Workshop**
- **Audacity Audiobook Editing Starter Class**
- **From Raw Voice to ACX-Ready Sample Workshop**
- **Home Voice-Over Recording Setup Class**

Use the existing price if one is already established. If not, choose a price appropriate to the current audience and delivery effort, using a modest low-ticket range such as **19–79** as a testing range rather than a permanent recommendation.

The buyer should leave with:

- A repeatable recording setup.
- A clean processing chain.
- A corrected sample.
- A checklist or project template.
- Confidence about what to fix next.

## Step 3: Audiobook Voice-Over Launchpad

This is the central flagship offer.

It should help a beginner move from:

> “I have a microphone and some recordings, but I do not know whether the audio is professional enough.”

to:

> “I can record, edit, process, check, and prepare audiobook or voice-over audio using a repeatable workflow.”

Possible components:

- Setup and room assessment.
- Recording levels.
- Audacity workflow.
- Noise and room-tone management.
- Editing breaths and silence gaps.
- Loudness and peak checks.
- ACX-oriented quality checks.
- Finished sample review.
- Workflow for completing a longer audiobook project.
- Feedback checkpoints.

Use the current or planned price. If pricing is not confirmed, test the offer with a placeholder such as **[Launchpad price]** rather than inventing a final number.

## Step 4: Premium Consulting

Premium Consulting is for people who need personal diagnosis rather than more general lessons.

Best-fit prospects include:

- An author whose audiobook audio keeps failing quality checks.
- A narrator who has tried several tutorials but still has inconsistent audio.
- Someone with room noise, harshness, clipping, or unstable levels.
- A client who wants a personal workflow audit.
- A Launchpad participant who needs individualized feedback.

Use the current consulting price or **[Consulting price]**. Do not discount it automatically. The value is the diagnosis, correction, and direct feedback.

# 4. Daily Minimum Operating Rhythm

Use this rhythm on most working days.

## Minimum version: 90 minutes

- **15 minutes:** Check sales, leads, email replies, and customer questions.
- **30 minutes:** Complete the day’s highest-priority revenue action.
- **30 minutes:** Create or improve content, email, sales material, or fulfillment.
- **15 minutes:** Record results and identify the next action.

## Standard version: 3–5 hours

- **30 minutes:** Metrics, customer replies, and follow-up.
- **90 minutes:** Content production or editing.
- **60 minutes:** Email, sales, or funnel work.
- **60 minutes:** Product, workshop, or client fulfillment.
- **30 minutes:** Documentation, tracking, and planning.

Do not allow YouTube production to consume all available time. Every week must include direct selling and follow-up.

# 5. Email Segmentation and Cadence

## Hot segment: approximately 500 subscribers

Include people who:

- Opened or clicked a recent campaign.
- Attended a recent class.
- Purchased previously.
- Replied to an email.
- Clicked a product or workshop link.
- Joined the list recently.

### Hot cadence

- 2 regular emails per week.
- Additional launch emails during a five- to seven-day promotion.
- Personal follow-up to people who click but do not purchase.
- One practical teaching email and one offer or invitation email each week.

## Dormant segment: approximately 800 subscribers

Start carefully. Do not send a hard sales campaign immediately.

### Dormant reactivation cadence

- Week 1: one “still want help?” email.
- Week 2: one useful checklist or audio mistake email.
- Week 3: one invitation to a short workshop or live event.
- Week 4: final re-engagement email.

If someone does not open or click after three to four reactivation emails, move them to a low-frequency segment or suppress them from regular campaigns.

### Reactivation question

Ask:

> What are you trying to do with your audio right now?

Use reply options such as:

- Record better voice-over audio.
- Produce an audiobook.
- Learn Audacity.
- Remove noise and hiss.
- Improve loudness and consistency.
- Get personal feedback.
- Something else.

Tag replies by problem, not merely by whether they opened an email.

# 10. Decision Rules

## If leads are below target

Check in this order:

1. Are the top videos visibly promoting the checklist?
2. Is the link above the fold in the description?
3. Is the pinned comment relevant to the video?
4. Does the landing page promise a specific audio result?
5. Is the CTA aimed at audiobook and voice-over beginners?
6. Are emails sending people to one clear next step?

Do not respond by creating an unrelated new lead magnet.

## If leads are healthy but starter-product sales are low

Check:

- Whether the product solves one urgent problem.
- Whether the result is demonstrated.
- Whether the checkout explains exactly what buyers receive.
- Whether the price reflects the format and support.
- Whether follow-up occurs after clicks.
- Whether people understand why the starter product is worth buying instead of watching another free video.

## If starter-product buyers do not move into Launchpad

Check:

- Whether the starter product ends with a clear next step.
- Whether Launchpad is presented as a complete workflow rather than “more lessons.”
- Whether buyers have a visible gap between one corrected sample and producing an entire audiobook or voice-over project.
- Whether the Launchpad page includes feedback, milestones, and proof.
- Whether buyers are receiving a personal follow-up email.

## If consulting calls are below target

Check:

- Whether videos explain when individualized diagnosis is needed.
- Whether the application is visible to people discussing complex audio problems.
- Whether the consulting promise is specific.
- Whether previous clients are described accurately.
- Whether the call-to-action is too broad.

## If calls happen but consulting sales are low

Check:

- Qualification before the call.
- Whether the client has a defined project and deadline.
- Whether the call spends too much time teaching for free.
- Whether the diagnosis, correction, and feedback process are clearly explained.
- Whether the price is being presented confidently and consistently.

## If YouTube views increase but revenue does not

The problem is likely not demand. Audit:

- CTA visibility.
- Landing-page conversion.
- Email follow-up.
- Product relevance.
- Tracking.
- Checkout friction.
- Offer transition.

## If revenue rises but delivery becomes unmanageable

- Reduce new content temporarily.
- Limit consulting slots.
- Use office hours or group feedback where appropriate.
- Improve onboarding and frequently asked questions.
- Preserve customer results before increasing promotion.

# 11. Recovery Rule for Missed Days

Do not restart the plan after missing a day.

Use this rule:

1. **Missed one day:** Complete the missed revenue action the next day and continue.
2. **Missed two or three days:** Keep only the highest-value action: email, sales follow-up, or fulfillment. Reschedule content.
3. **Missed a full week:** Do not cram seven days into one weekend. Re-enter at the nearest weekly review, update the dashboard, and choose the one funnel bottleneck to address.
4. **Never skip fulfillment or warm-lead follow-up** to produce another YouTube video.
5. If energy is low, complete the 90-minute minimum rhythm:
   - Reply to leads.
   - Send one useful email.
   - Improve one CTA or sales page section.
   - Record the result.

The plan succeeds through repeated contact with existing demand, clear audio-production outcomes, and measured improvement—not through publishing more random tutorials.
